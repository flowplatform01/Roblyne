# Evenum — Participation Payment, Candidate Experience & Financial UX Refinement

## Context

This refinement continues improving the production platform.

Do not rebuild existing systems.

Review the current implementation and refine weak product decisions, incomplete payment experiences, and rigid workflows.

The objective is to make participation, monetization, and financial interactions feel natural, fair, configurable, and production-ready.

---

# Mission

Participation should feel effortless.

Payment should feel transparent.

Candidates should become first-class public entities.

Organizations should have control over financial behaviour without compromising fairness.

All refinements must integrate with the existing Commission Engine and financial architecture.

---

# 1. Remove Internal Infrastructure References

The public interface must never expose internal platform infrastructure.

Review all payment experiences.

Remove wording that references internal services such as payment gateways.

Examples include labels such as:

* "Pay with Flow Pay"

Replace them with user-focused language such as:

* Pay
* Continue to Payment
* Complete Payment
* Confirm Payment

Internal payment providers belong behind the platform architecture, not in the customer experience.

---

# 2. Configurable Participant Visibility

Organizations should decide whether participant information is publicly visible.

Support configurable options such as:

* Show participant count
* Hide participant count
* Show contributor identities
* Hide contributor identities
* Show supporter list
* Keep participation private

Privacy should be configurable at the organization or experience level depending on the pillar.

The platform should never assume participant visibility by default.

---

# 3. Fair Voting Payment Model

Review the paid voting architecture.

Payment configuration must remain fair.

Supported pricing levels:

* Entire election
* Individual position

Do **not** support pricing per candidate.

Candidates competing for the same position must always participate under identical financial conditions.

This preserves fairness and avoids introducing economic bias into elections.

---

# 4. Candidate Profile as Participation Entry Point

Every candidate should have a dedicated public profile.

The profile becomes more than a biography.

It becomes the primary participation experience.

The page should present:

* Candidate identity
* Position
* Organization
* Manifesto
* Biography
* Campaign media
* Election information
* Share functionality
* Voting actions

If paid voting is enabled, the page should allow participation directly without requiring unnecessary navigation back through the election wizard, this is very important .

---

# 5. Flexible Vote Quantity Experience

Current implementation assumes one payment for one voting action.

Improve this workflow.

When paid voting is enabled:

Allow participants to specify how many votes they wish to purchase.

Examples:

5 votes

10 votes

25 votes

50 votes

100 votes

Or another permitted quantity according to the organization's configuration.

The platform should immediately calculate:

* Price per vote
* Selected quantity
* Total amount payable

The relationship between payment and voting entitlement should always be clear before payment begins.

---

# 6. Intelligent Payment Summary

Before payment confirmation, clearly present:

Selected candidate

Selected position

Vote quantity

Price per vote

Total votes

Total payable amount

Applicable currency

The summary should eliminate ambiguity and build trust.

---

# 7. Currency Management

Do not assume a single currency.

Support organization-configured currencies.

The selected currency must propagate consistently throughout:

Payment screens

Receipts

Transaction history

Financial reports

Organization dashboards

Participant confirmations

The platform should display the appropriate currency for each organization rather than using a universal default.

---

# 8. Shareable Candidate Experiences

Candidate pages should support professional sharing.

Shared links should open directly to the intended candidate.

If voting is available, participants should be guided naturally into the participation flow.

Sharing should improve discoverability while preserving a smooth experience.

---

# 9. UX Refinement

Review every payment interaction.

Reduce unnecessary steps.

Avoid repeated navigation.

Provide immediate feedback.

Keep payment progression obvious.

Allow users to understand what they are purchasing before committing.

The experience should feel deliberate, transparent, and effortless.

---

# 10. Platform Consistency

Apply these refinements consistently wherever financial participation exists.

Voting

Events

Campaigns

Future participation modules

Every financial experience should follow the same product philosophy while respecting the unique behaviour of each pillar.

---

# Final Objective

After this refinement:

* Public interfaces should no longer expose internal payment infrastructure.
* Organizations should control participant visibility according to their needs.
* Paid voting should support fair pricing at the election or position level, never per candidate.
* Every candidate should have a rich, shareable public profile that also serves as a participation entry point.
* Participants should be able to purchase multiple votes through a clear, quantity-based payment flow rather than repeating small transactions.
* Currency should respect each organization's configuration and be presented consistently across the platform.
* Payment, participation, and candidate discovery should feel seamless, transparent, and production-ready.

The implementation should strengthen Evenum's identity as a professional participation platform where fairness, clarity, configurability, and user experience  are treated as core product principles rather than optional enhancements.

---

# 11. Platform-Controlled Payment Experience & Currency Governance

## Payment Experience Configuration

The voting payment experience should not be hardcoded.

Instead, introduce a platform-level configuration managed exclusively by the Platform Administrator.

This configuration determines how participants interact with paid voting across the platform.

Support two presentation modes.

### Amount-First Experience

Participants enter the amount they wish to pay.

The platform immediately calculates and displays:

* Number of votes received
* Price per vote
* Total voting entitlement
* Confirmation before payment

Example:

User enters **5,000 XAF**

↓

Platform automatically displays:

"You will receive 50 votes."

↓

Proceed to payment.

This experience feels natural for participants who already know how much they wish to contribute.

---

### Quantity-First Experience

Participants choose how many votes they want.

The platform immediately calculates:

* Price per vote
* Total payable amount

Example:

User selects:

50 votes

↓

Platform displays:

"Total payment: 5,000 XAF"

↓

Proceed to payment.

This experience is more appropriate where voting is treated as purchasing voting units.

---

### Platform Governance

The Platform Administrator should determine which payment experience is active.

Organizations should not configure this behaviour individually.

The goal is to provide a consistent participation experience across the entire Evenum platform while still allowing the platform operator to choose the interaction model that best suits its market.

This configuration should remain extensible for future participation models without requiring changes to the voting architecture.

---

## Currency Governance

Currency management should also be controlled at the platform level.

Organizations should not independently define currencies.

Instead, the Platform Administrator manages:

* Supported currencies
* Default platform currency
* Currency availability
* Currency activation and deactivation
* Future payment provider compatibility

Organizations may only use currencies that have been enabled by the platform.

For the current deployment, configure **XAF** as the default operational currency and ensure all payment interfaces, financial calculations, receipts, summaries, reports, dashboards, and participant experiences consistently display XAF.

Design the architecture so that additional currencies can be enabled later by the Platform Administrator without requiring changes to business logic.

---

## Design Principle

Payment should always feel simple and trustworthy.

Participants should never need to calculate conversions or estimate outcomes themselves.

Regardless of the configured payment mode, the interface should immediately communicate:

* The amount being paid
* The number of votes received
* The applicable currency
* The total participation entitlement
* The final payment confirmation

The experience should eliminate ambiguity and create confidence before payment is completed.
